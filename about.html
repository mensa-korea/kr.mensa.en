About Mensa Korea

Thirty years of bringing people together in Korea.

What Mensa is

The word mensa means table in Latin. The name represents a round-table society in which race, color, creed, national origin, age, politics, education, and social background are irrelevant.

Mensa was founded in England in 1946 by Roland Berrill, a barrister, and Dr. Lance Ware, a scientist and lawyer. They envisioned a society for highly intelligent people with a single membership criterion: a qualifying IQ score. From the beginning, Mensa was intended to be nonpolitical and free from racial and religious distinctions.

The society welcomes people from every walk of life whose scores place them in the top 2% of the general population. Members enjoy one another's company and take part in a wide range of social and cultural activities. Mensa now has about 150,000 members in more than 100 countries.

Mensa Korea

Mensa activity in Korea began in July 1996 through the efforts of Edward Vincent, then executive director of Mensa International, and Hyojin Ahn, an international Mensa member. More than 500 people joined within the first year through regular testing, and those members went on to form Special Interest Groups that remain central to the community.

The first board of directors took office in August 1997, and Mensa Korea was formally launched at COEX in January 1998. In October 2005, Mensa International recognized it as a full National Mensa. Today, Mensa Korea has about 3,000 members nationwide, across Seoul and four regional branches.

Mensa Korea is a nonprofit society. Its purposes are the same as those of Mensa everywhere: to identify and foster human intelligence for the benefit of humanity, to encourage research into the nature, characteristics, and uses of intelligence, and to provide a stimulating intellectual and social environment for its members.

Timeline

  1. 1995

    Edward Vincent, executive director of Mensa International, and Hyojin Ahn, an international Mensa member, begin preparing the first Mensa test in Korea.

  2. 1996

    The first admission test is held at the 63 Building in Seoul on July 6. A preliminary issue of the Mensa Korea Journal follows in December.

  3. 1997

    The first member orientation takes place in May, the first board of directors is elected in August, and the first full journal is published in October.

  4. 1998

    Mensa Korea is formally launched at a founding assembly at COEX in Seoul on January 17, with 234 people present. The official website goes online in June.

  5. 2000

    Mensa Korea is registered as an incorporated association.

  6. 2005

    Mensa International recognizes Mensa Korea as a full National Mensa.

  7. 2007

    The Seoul office opens in Jamwon-dong and the monthly gathering (MKMG) series begins.

  8. 2008–2014

    Regional branches are established for Daejeon–Chungcheong, Busan–Ulsan–Gyeongnam, Daegu–Gyeongbuk–Gangwon, and Gwangju–Honam–Jeju.

  9. 2015

    A special test is held on Jeju Island for the first time.

  10. 2019–2022

    The 12th, 13th, and 14th boards are elected; testing and gatherings continue through the pandemic years.

  11. 2025

    The 15th board of directors is elected on February 19.

  12. 2026

    A dedicated test and orientation site opens, testing resumes for applicants age 14 and older, the lifetime cap of three attempts is removed, and the Korean website moves to mensa.kr.

  13. 2027

    Mensa Korea will host the International Board of Directors meeting of Mensa International.
